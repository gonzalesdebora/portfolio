Umbrella Chair

Industrial Design, 2018

Challenge

Diseño de Mobiliario: Visiones Nórdicas was a hands-on summer furniture design course at Universidad de los Andes, led by Paola Cabrera and Heikki Ruoho, built around a single deliberately narrow brief, to design an object for sitting using nothing but 6mm smooth steel rod, able to hold an adult's weight, within roughly 800×800×800mm. The approach was Nordic-Finnish, prioritizing function and accessibility over ornament and historicism, and learning entirely by making rather than by rendering.

Solution

Working in a team of three, we developed the Umbrella Chair through the course's "sketching in the air" method, bending and welding steel rod directly into three-dimensional form, testing structural ideas physically rather than on paper, and refining the design through iteration and trial and error until it held weight reliably within the material's single-gauge constraint. I also joined the three-person graphics team designing the identity and materials for "Sketching in the Air," the exhibition presenting all 25 students' resulting chairs.

Success

The Umbrella Chair was exhibited alongside 24 other steel-rod seating designs in "Sketching in the Air: Experiments for Sitting" at Universidad de los Andes' Facultad de Arquitectura y Diseño, opening July 19, 2017.
