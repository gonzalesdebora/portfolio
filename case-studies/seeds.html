Arati

UX Research

Challenge

Arati had sketched out three early "seeds" of a wellness ecosystem for older adults in Colombia — a life-mentorship app, an in-home services app, and a collective-buying-power benefits card — but none had been tested with real users. The target audience skews toward low digital literacy and low trust in online tools, so it wasn't clear which, if any, of the three concepts would actually work.

Solution

We ran 36 in-depth interviews across Bogotá, Medellín, and Barranquilla, testing all three low-fidelity prototypes with older adults and their adult children. The same patterns surfaced everywhere: a strong preference for WhatsApp and phone calls over app self-service, and distrust of digital payments. We translated those patterns into six required attributes — familiarity, simplicity, flexibility, trust, omnichannel, closeness — and used them to reshape three disconnected apps into one integrated model built around levels of autonomy.

Success

The research reframed three unvalidated concepts into one adaptable ecosystem spanning five life areas, directly shaping the next design phase. Follow-on digital experiments — landing pages, Facebook campaigns, and 100-person surveys — validated demand at scale: one Facebook campaign alone generated 71 leads for the in-home-services concept, giving the business case that followed a validated model instead of a guess.
